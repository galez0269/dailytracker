## Industry Buzz

**ChatGPT is about to show brands their mention count**  ·  `MUST-KNOW`
- **Source:** [Digiday — ChatGPT is about to show brands their mention count](https://digiday.com/marketing/chatgpt-is-about-to-show-brands-their-mention-count/), 9 Nov 2026 — Krystal Scanlon
- **What:** OpenAI is testing an analytics view inside a "Business Hub" that has not launched yet. It charts, day by day, how often ChatGPT mentions a business in its answers and how many clicks those mentions send to the business's site. Access is tied to a "claim your business" step that needs proof of ownership (for example, linking a Shopify store), and claimed businesses also get a business agent that answers customer questions. It will not show prompts, user data or competitor rankings, so it is much narrower than Google's search tools. The feature was spotted from a banner screenshot (Marketplace Pulse); OpenAI declined to comment. Digiday notes the dashboard could feed OpenAI's ad business: a brand watching a number it wants to raise is a likely buyer of sponsored placements. New since the 11-05 ChatGPT-ads and 11-06 Amazon-agent must-knows: the AI platforms now plan to grade brands as well as sell them space.
- **Why us:** Once there is a number, clients will chase it. Our job is to say what actually moves it: being talked about by communities, reviewed and recommended by creators, and covered in the press, which is Proof built up over time, not a content fix. Get a simple point of view ready on how to read a mention count (and why it should not be bought up with ads alone) before clients start reporting it upward. Unreleased; details rest on a screenshot and unnamed sources.

**How performance metrics are rewriting creator rate cards**  ·  `MUST-KNOW`
- **Source:** [Digiday — How performance metrics are rewriting creator rate cards](https://digiday.com/marketing/how-performance-metrics-are-rewriting-creator-rate-cards/), 9 Nov 2026 — Kimeko McCoy
- **What:** Brands increasingly want cost per lead, cost per conversion, earned media value and ROAS before agreeing a creator fee; some ask up front for Instagram link clicks and engaged YouTube views. Talent managers describe tighter sales accountability; one says every client now has a cost-per-conversion benchmark (each one different). Some agencies review a creator's last 30 days and strip out viral outliers; others treat views as one input beside audience fit and shares. YouTube's August change to count views from the moment playback starts makes comparisons harder. Rothy's is cited as taking a looser approach, prioritising fit with the brand. AI dashboards now show a creator's engagement and conversion history, and follower count is becoming a vanity metric.
- **Why us:** We know creators, so we should own the balanced answer: price creators on what they deliver, but measure two things, sales now and brand memory later. Pure cost-per-conversion pricing pushes brands toward creators who sell well this week, not ones who build the brand. That is the compounding argument applied to the rate card. Follows the 11-05 Digiday Creator Marketing Index and 11-06 Joellen Lu "views per post" items.

**Ogilvy and CAA team up on AI-generated celebrity ads, with Terry Crews lending his likeness**  ·  `HIGH-IMPACT`
- **Source:** [The New York Times — A.I.-Generated Celebrity Ads Get an Assist From Terry Crews](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/09/business/media/ogilvy-caa-ai-ads.html), 9 Nov 2026 (blocked to our fetcher; headline and standfirst only); background: [Ogilvy — Bridging the AI trust gap](https://www.ogilvy.com/ideas/bridging-ai-trust-gap-how-brands-talent-can-navigate-new-frontier), 9 Oct 2026
- **What:** Per the NYT standfirst, Ogilvy and the talent agency CAA have launched a partnership to make ads using digital replicas of celebrities. An October Ogilvy panel with CAA and Crews set out the principles: talent opt in, own and approve their likeness, are paid, and AI content is labelled; CAA already stores clients' digital likenesses in its "Vault". Secondary reports name the product "ECHO" and say brands could license likenesses without the talent on set; we could not confirm those details at source.
- **Why us:** Lands on two threads at once: the 11-06 "Christmas went to celebrities" must-know (brands borrowing fame) and the 11-05/11-06 handmade items (audiences now check whether work is real). A licensed celebrity replica is legal, but it may not feel real to audiences. Our angle: consent fixes the legal side but not trust, and audiences already reward visible human effort. Creators, who show up in person, may become the "real" option next to synthetic celebrities. Confirm terms before citing externally.

**Talarico's Texas Senate win: 3 billion organic views, with paid money still mostly on broadcast**  ·  `HIGH-IMPACT`
- **Source:** [Adweek — Pastor Talarico, 3 Billion Views, and $56 Million on Broadcast: How James Talarico's Team Flipped Texas](https://www.adweek.com/media/pastor-talarico-3-billion-views-and-56-million-on-broadcast-how-james-talaricos-team-flipped-texas/), 6 Nov 2026 (date from feed; subscriber-only, intro and key takeaways seen)
- **What:** The campaign says its organic social video earned 3B+ views since September 2025, with clips of Talarico in committee hearings going viral on TikTok. Its paid budget went mostly to broadcast ($56.5M), then CTV ($8.3M) and YouTube ($8M); most other digital spend was left to outside groups. It merged speechwriting and social into one team and treated vertical video as storytelling, and credits TikTok and YouTube with a near 50-point swing among men aged 18–44 (campaign-reported).
- **Why us:** A social-first case outside brand marketing: organic social built the character and broadcast bought the reach. The lesson for clients is the org chart: the people who write the message and the people who make social sat in one team. Follows 10-30 creators-in-politics and 11-05 Gen Z midterms items; campaign figures not independently checked.

**Google makes AI Overviews more link-friendly just as publishers get the right to opt out**  ·  `SIGNAL`
- **Source:** [Digiday — As publishers await click data, Google's AI visibility push complicates the opt-out decision](https://digiday.com/media/as-publishers-await-click-data-googles-ai-visibility-push-complicates-the-opt-out-decision/), 9 Nov 2026
- **What:** Following the UK CMA ruling, Google now lets sites worldwide opt out of its AI features (as one bundle, not feature by feature), and must give UK publishers click data by 3 Dec. Meanwhile it has updated AI Mode and AI Overviews to show links more often and more prominently, with article carousels. Publishers are holding off on opting out until they see clicks.
- **Why us:** Same theme as the ChatGPT dashboard: the AI platforms are starting to share visibility data, and clicks are the number everyone is waiting for. Log for the AEO thread.
