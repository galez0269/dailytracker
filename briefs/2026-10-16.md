## Industry Buzz

**Microdrama goes mainstream, without a playbook**  ·  `MUST-KNOW`
- **Source:** [Digiday — Ownership, control, margins: the brand playbook for microseries is still taking shape](https://digiday.com/media/ownership-control-margins-the-brand-playbook-for-microseries-is-still-taking-shape/), Oct 16 2026; [Marketing Dive (Social Media Today) — TikTok inks microdrama partnership with Made By Us studios](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/sociable-tiktok-inks-microdrama-partnership-with-made-by-us-studios/833115/), Oct 15; [Feed Me — Banana Republic's scripted microseries did not shock me](https://www.readfeedme.com/p/banana-republics-scripted-microseries), Oct 15 (paywalled, intro only); [Adweek — Why Branded Entertainment Goes Unwatched](https://www.adweek.com/creativity/why-brand-entertainment-goes-unwatched-and-what-to-do-about-it/), Oct 2026 (opinion by Chris Watling, Baby Teeth; paywalled)
- **What:** Brands have two routes. They can partner with an existing hit, which comes with distribution, or build their own, which is cheaper and gives more control. Examples include Alexis Bittar's mockumentary, SharkNinja's *Brooklyn Coffee Shop*, and Nuuly's dating series with no call to action, which drew millions of TikTok views. One producer puts the average budget at about $200K per season. Scripted creators such as Gray Fagan (5M followers) keep the rights to their characters. TikTok gets exclusive rights to selected Made By Us series for TikTok and its PineDrama app, and says its HOORAE series *Screen Time* has drawn nearly 580M views. Gap Inc. now has a chief entertainment officer. Adweek's opinion piece warns that most branded entertainment fails because nobody plans its distribution, not because the ideas are bad.
- **Why us:** Following yesterday's Dentsu item, this is the clearest sign yet that entertainment as reserve currency (Q2 Review) is becoming a budget line. The open questions are where we have the answers: whether to cast a creator or a celebrity, who owns the IP, and how to build the series for TikTok and Reels distribution from the start. A short social-first microdrama POV (cast creators, plan distribution natively, measure Proof rather than views) would land well now. Entertainment priority category; Think Forward.

**Creator budgets grow while measurement lags**  ·  `MUST-KNOW`
- **Source:** [Digiday — Ruggable's new ad spend strategy shifts focus to ShopMy creators](https://digiday.com/marketing/ruggables-new-ad-spend-strategy-shifts-focus-to-shopmy-creators/), Oct 16 2026; [Marketing Dive — Why brands struggle with influencer measurement — and what to do about it](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/why-brands-struggle-with-influencer-measurement-and-what-to-do-about-it/832380/), Oct 15; corroborated by [Marketing Dive — What marketers need to know from Advertising Week New York 2026](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/what-marketers-need-to-know-from-advertising-week-new-york-2026/833139/), Oct 16
- **What:** Ruggable has moved about 10% of its spend from Meta and other performance channels into brand channels (creators and CTV) and plans to grow brand spend 15–20% next year. It moved more than $1M from Meta into ShopMy creators last holiday, and ShopMy revenue reportedly hit about $3M in its first month. Its CMO says that "what got us here is not going to get us there". An ANA report finds two-thirds of marketers rank measurement as the hardest part of creator marketing. eMarketer expects spend on amplifying creator content to match creator-sponsored revenue (about $14.2B) in 2027 and then overtake it. Kantar finds 66% of brands plan to invest in micro creators against 41% for macro. At AWNY, Chili's, Disney and Alaska Airlines said deeper creator deals improve efficiency.
- **Why us:** Creator marketing is turning into brand-building with paid media behind it, and the measurement gap is the opening. Clients need someone who can run the creator relationship, the amplification and the brand-outcome measurement as one system. ANA's own advice to bring creator relationships under one owner is the case for us. This is the core Creator Report argument and extends yesterday's partner-vs-media split.

**WARC: consumer goods giants keep raising ad intensity as others cut**  ·  `HIGH-IMPACT`
- **Source:** [Marketing Dive — Consumer goods giants step up ad intensity as other big spenders pull back: report](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/consumer-goods-giants-step-up-ad-intensity-as-other-big-spenders-pull-back/833102/), Oct 15 2026 (WARC Media analysis)
- **What:** Top consumer goods marketers raised their ad-to-sales ratio from 11.6% (2023) to 12.5% (2025), and their ad spend grew 36% from 2019 to 2025 against 29% revenue growth. Tech companies cut their ratio from 13% to 7.4% while revenue grew 146%. WARC calls it a "two-speed" ad market. Of the six consumer goods companies studied, PepsiCo was the only one not to raise its ratio.
- **Why us:** It's a headline stat for the FMCG Report, which is due this month: FMCG is still the category that pays to build brands. Our job is to show where that growing budget compounds on social rather than buying reach in spikes.

**Social commerce overtakes SEO at The Independent**  ·  `HIGH-IMPACT`

## Trends

**Paris Fashion Week's attention record was almost all Dior, through Thai fandom**  ·  `HIGH-IMPACT`
- **Source:** [Glossy — Luxury Briefing: Dior drove Paris Fashion Week's record buzz](https://www.glossy.co/fashion/luxury/luxury-briefing-dior-drove-paris-fashion-weeks-record-buzz/), Oct 16 2026 (Lefty x Karla Otto data)
- **What:** SS27 Paris Fashion Week generated $795.8M in earned media value, up 59% year on year. Dior accounted for $360M of that and 91% of the growth. Two Thai actresses, Orm Kornnaphat and Lingling Kwong, generated 56% of Dior's figure. Without Dior, growth was 6.3%, and 34 of the 62 brands tracked lost EMV. EMV estimates the value of the exposure, not sales.
- **Why us:** Luxury's attention comes from fandoms, not front rows: Asian talent with devoted, highly organised fans. That's Presence and Participation earned through communities we understand. It's a strong luxury-pitch stat and a fandom case for the Creator Report. The caveat to make is that concentrated EMV isn't the same as compounding desirability.

## POVs

**ANU vs WGSN: top-down trend forecasting is losing to trend strategy**  ·  `HIGH-IMPACT`
- **Source:** [WHAT'S ANU — Is The World's Largest Trend Forecasting Company Trying to Intimidate Me?](https://whatsanu.substack.com/p/is-the-worlds-largest-trend-forecasting), Oct 15 2026 — Anu Lingala
- **What:** WGSN wrote to ANU and Alexi Gunner (idle gaze) claiming their work relied on WGSN's proprietary content. ANU calls the claim baseless and says WGSN appeared to back down. ANU argues that culture and trends aren't intellectual property. Forecasting isn't dead, but a single top-down direction loses value as culture fragments into microtrends. What replaces it is bespoke "trend strategy" that combines foresight, macrotrends and brand strategy.
- **Why us:** We agree, and it describes our cultural insights offer: read culture first-hand and turn it into brand strategy, rather than resell a forecast. Useful framing for Think Forward and for positioning our insights capability against the trend-subscription model. The open-culture point also matters for how we credit sources.
