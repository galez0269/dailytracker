## Industry Buzz

**GTA VI: the week's biggest entertainment moment, and the pressure to bandwagon**  ·  `MUST-KNOW`
- **Source:** [Dazed — 'It's more fun than work': Meet the people booking time off to play GTA VI](https://www.dazeddigital.com/life-culture/article/71349/1/meet-the-legends-booking-time-off-work-to-play-grand-theft-auto-gta-vi), 12 Nov 2026; [Adweek — Why Your Brand Needs a Gaming Strategy](https://www.adweek.com/brand-marketing/why-your-brand-needs-a-gaming-strategy/), 16 Nov 2026 (opinion, Chris Erb; partly paywalled)
- **What:** GTA VI releases on Thursday 19 Nov. Dazed reports it is the most pre-ordered game in PlayStation history, about 83,000 UK gamers have booked time off (BrightHR estimates it will cost employers over £12M), Selfridges has a dedicated pop-up, fans have organised a GTA V "one last ride" event on 18 Nov, and there is an IRL festival in Miami with Travis Scott and Cardi B. In Adweek, former EA Sports brand VP Chris Erb calls it a "culturally destabilizing event" and argues brands need a real gaming strategy, not a one-off tie-in.
- **Why us:** Entertainment as reserve currency (Q2 review) in its purest form. Our line for clients: don't buy a moment in GTA, show up where its community already is, with streamers, clips and the rituals around the launch (time off, watch parties), and keep showing up after the week ends. That is participation that compounds, and it fits yesterday's Twitch item. Most figures are from Dazed and its sources (BrightHR), not Rockstar.

**Nobody agrees what a "creator" is, and it is getting in the way of pricing and measurement**  ·  `HIGH-IMPACT`
- **Source:** [Digiday — What exactly counts as a creator? The industry still can't agree](https://digiday.com/marketing/what-exactly-counts-as-a-creator-the-industry-still-cant-agree/), 17 Nov 2026; [Digiday — Anatomy of an influencer marketing deal](https://digiday.com/future-of-tv/anatomy-of-an-influencer-marketing-deal/), 17 Nov 2026
- **What:** General Mills, Shutterfly and Rothy's each use different creator tiers (by followers, by "cultural relevance", by funnel role), and the IAB finds at least 23% of marketers want a shared taxonomy. VML's Noah Eisemann argues for defining creators by the role they play in a campaign (salesperson, ambassador, product collaborator). In a separate podcast, YSL Beauty's influence lead says its creator work usually carries no links and aims for a "virality moment" other creators pick up; brands ask for cross-posting to three platforms and two weeks of paid usage up front, and talent managers trade those rights against fee.
- **Why us:** Whitespace: a creator taxonomy built on role and cultural relevance, not follower count, is something we could publish. It also answers yesterday's Unilever 500K-creator benchmark: you cannot value or compound creator work you cannot classify. Creator marketing is a core capability we are elevating.

**Beauty leaders on GEO and TikTok Shop: measure AI visibility first; unpaid affiliates beat paid boosting**  ·  `HIGH-IMPACT`
- **Source:** [Glossy — How beauty and wellness leaders are prioritizing GEO and TikTok Shop](https://www.glossy.co/beauty/how-beauty-and-wellness-leaders-are-prioritizing-geo-and-tiktok-shop/), 17 Nov 2026; context: [Glossy — Beauty Briefing: 'Dune: Part Three'… (TikTok Shop pilots a paid membership tier)](https://www.glossy.co/beauty/beauty-briefing-dune-part-three-is-poised-to-be-a-box-office-sensation-can-beauty-brands-cash-in-on-the-hype/), 17 Nov 2026 (members only; standfirst)
- **What:** At Glossy's summit town hall (9–11 Nov, speakers unnamed), a women's-health brand said AI chatbots spread wrong information about its category and advised measuring AI visibility before changing anything; another leader said to put more effort into GEO and let it feed SEO. Beauty on TikTok Shop is described as a $2B business. One brand said paid boosting on TikTok Shop "did not generate the revenue we wanted, nor… awareness"; what worked was organic virality from two affiliates who asked for the product unpaid, with a halo to DTC, Amazon and Ulta. TikTok Shop is also piloting a paid membership tier.
- **Why us:** Two of our arguments in one room: AI answers are shaped by what real people say (follows the 11-16 AEO must-know), and on TikTok Shop real enthusiasm beats paid reach. It backs a creator-first, community-first approach to social commerce with clients' own words. Anonymous speakers, so use as colour, not proof.

## Trends

**Attention sold back: brainstacking, phone blockers and phone-free shows**  ·  `MUST-KNOW`
- **Source:** [Dazed — Could brainstacking fix your fried attention span?](https://www.dazeddigital.com/life-culture/article/71314/1/could-brainstacking-fix-your-fried-attention-span-tiktok-wellness-trend-viral), 13 Nov 2026; [After School — Code Rot and Spirited Lads](https://afterschool.substack.com/p/code-rot-and-spirited-lads), 13 Nov 2026; [After School — Surveillance Chic and Lore Chasing](https://afterschool.substack.com/p/surveillance-chic-and-lore-chasing), 16 Nov 2026 (paid, intro only)
- **What:** Brainstacking (lining up reading, puzzles and journaling to replace scrolling, filmed and posted) is credited to LibraryTok creator Mandy Rulez, whose accounts gained over 150,000 followers in October; psychologists quoted by Dazed see no clear cognitive benefit. After School, summarising a Dazed piece, lists products that sell attention back (Steppin, PhoneLess, Brick, whose searches rose 250% in a year, phone-free cold-plunge spas) and notes a viral "un-rot" week left one 22-year-old's screen time "a lot worse." Phoebe Bridgers has announced a phone-free North American arena tour for 2027, and After School's Monday read leads with "analogmaxxing."
- **Why us:** New since the 10-28 brainstacking and 11-03 scroll-cutting items: escaping the feed is now a market, and people perform the escape on the feed. That is a Think Forward macro shift with a clear implication: brands that give people something worth their full attention (series, events, objects) earn credit; brands that add to the scroll lose it. Unfakeable capital: time spent offline with a brand is hard to fake.

- **What:** Google and Samsung opened preorders on 12 Nov for Gemini glasses designed by Warby Parker (from $395) and Gentle Monster (from $449), both with cameras. Launch marketing split three ways: Gentle Monster with a Jonathan Glazer film starring Joaquin Phoenix, Warby Parker with a literary pop-up, HTC with creator day-in-the-life posts. Young people Glossy spoke to treat the glasses as a content tool for themselves but assume anyone wearing them is recording; one said choosing what you share differs from someone else deciding for you.
- **Why us:** Follows the surveillance thread (08-04 surveillance panic, 07-21 "reclaiming surveillance"). Platform-feature fluency: first-person creator content will grow, and consent will be part of the brief, especially at events. A point of view on "filming with permission" is an easy one for us to own.

**CeraVe's playbook: start social-first, then go full media, with local creators doing the translation**  ·  `HIGH-IMPACT`
- **Source:** [Marketing Dive — How CeraVe's 'Dune' deal takes the brand's social-first playbook global](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/how-ceraves-dune-deal-takes-the-brands-social-first-playbook-global/874654/), 16 Nov 2026 — Vincent Chauvière, global brand president, CeraVe
- **What:** CeraVe is "Official Moisturizer of Arrakis" for "Dune: Part Three" (out 18 Dec), its first film deal, made by OBB Bolded. Chauvière says the campaign is "social-first, starting with a bit of excitement and surprise, and then we are going full media", with local dermatologists and creators in each market because "the influencer world is a local world." CeraVe's "medutainment" (science plus entertainment) helped build a $2B brand; its summer CerAwards gathered 100+ creators from 22 countries and generated nearly 60M views (company figures). Glossy reports other beauty brands (Glamnetic) also tying into Dune.
- **Why us:** Agree and extend. Read with yesterday's Unilever (volume) and Guinness (go slow): this is a third model, one global entertainment idea that local creators translate. Entertainment as reserve currency, made to compound through a standing creator community. A clear social-first case to cite.

**MØRNING: youth culture magazines have started to read like ads**  ·  `HIGH-IMPACT`
- **Source:** [MØRNING (Burn After Reading) — Quick Fire: Have Youth Subculture Magazines Lost The Plot?](https://morningfyi.substack.com/p/quick-fire-have-youth-subculture), 13 Nov 2026 — Ora Margolis
- **What:** Margolis argues that youth titles that began as scrappy zines now run celebrity covers, media-trained interviews and spreads of the same big-group looks, and that readers want first-hand reporting from inside subcultures, not "glorified ads."
- **Why us:** Agree. It is the unfakeable-capital argument applied to media: access bought through PR is not insight. It supports our cultural-insights pitch (we get our read from communities directly) and is a caution for brand partnerships with youth titles. Possible guest voice for Think Forward.
