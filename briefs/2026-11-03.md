## Industry Buzz

**Reddit's AI-search hit: referrals down 1M+ a week, US daily users fall, logged-in experience comes first**  ·  `MUST-KNOW`
- **Source:** [Marketing Dive — What Reddit's Q3 earnings mean for marketers amid major traffic shift](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/what-reddits-q3-earnings-mean-for-marketers-amid-major-traffic-shift/873386/), 2 Nov 2026 (earnings reported 29 Oct)
- **What:** Reddit's Q3 revenue rose 53% to $895M and ad revenue 54% to $847M (down from 74% in Q1 and 64% in Q2). US daily users fell from 53.2M to 50.7M, international rose to 79.6M, and total daily users were flat. Referral traffic dropped by more than a million a week on average in October; the CEO blames changes in how Google's AI Mode handles results, and the shareholder letter warns daily users could fall by a low double-digit percentage next quarter if the trend holds. Reddit says it is moving to a logged-in-first experience because logged-in users drive most engagement and ad value (Marketing Dive's wording is "logged-in only"; secondary reports describe a Q4 test in select non-US markets, so the scope is not settled). Reddit's COO says half of US shoppers verify AI recommendations on Reddit, which is Reddit's own claim.
- **Why us:** Think Forward: a macro shift in where trust gets built and checked. AI answers draw on community proof (Reddit was the top ChatGPT citation source in the 09-16 item), yet the open-web traffic that fed those communities is draining, so the proof layer is moving behind logins. Brand implication for the LLM-visibility question: community presence is an input to AI answers, not only a reach channel, and it increasingly lives in gated spaces we have to be in, not just near. Extends the 11-02 Alexa-and-Google must-know from "usage" to "what happens to the sources".

**Publishers push back on Meta's AI-scraping terms but say they cannot afford to leave**  ·  `HIGH-IMPACT`
- **Source:** [Digiday — 'They hold us hostage': Publishers furious over terms change still can't quit Facebook](https://digiday.com/media/they-hold-us-hostage-publishers-furious-over-terms-change-still-cant-quit-facebook/), 3 Nov 2026
- **What:** What changed since the 10-28 item on Meta's delayed terms: publisher trade groups (News Media Alliance, Digital Content Next, UK's PPA) say they have not been consulted on terms that would let Meta use their content, including for AI scraping, in return for platform access. Regulators and lawmakers are circling (a UK CMA investigation, a Norwegian court injunction, US senators including Adam Schiff). The leverage problem: Meta averaged 3.47% of publisher traffic in July (Chatbeat) and Facebook traffic to the top 10 publisher sites fell 5% year on year (Similarweb), yet publishers still rely on it for branded content, paid media and subscriptions. The PPA wants access unbundled from permission to crawl. The article gives no effective date; the 20 Nov date comes from the earlier item.
- **Why us:** Brands using Meta's AI creative tools are on the other side of the same terms (advertiser liability and watermarking), so this is a live client question with a date attached. It also shows the platform-bargain pattern from the Google crawler threat (08-06) repeating on social: dependence beats principle.

**Agency strategists: optimism about AI is up, but so is the fear of replacement; clients are sending AI-written briefs**  ·  `HIGH-IMPACT`
- **Source:** [Marketing Dive — Where agency strategists see opportunity as AI challenges their worth](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/where-agency-strategists-see-opportunity-as-ai-challenges-their-worth/872985/), 2 Nov 2026 (WARC Future of Strategy 2026); [Digiday — Media Buying Briefing: Overheard at Media Buying Summit](https://digiday.com/media-buying/media-buying-briefing-overheard-at-media-buying-summit-how-ai-is-helping-and-hurting-client-relations-and-office-work/), 2 Nov 2026
- **What:** New since the 07-30 "crossroads" call for the same survey: WARC's results from 489 agency strategists (fielded in August) show 66% see reasons for optimism and 65% say AI frees time for more nuanced work, but 56% worry AI could eventually replace them (41% last year), 80% say they need stronger proof of business impact and 57% need a new value proposition and pay model; 50% report client influence (41% in 2025). Marketing Dive adds that 6,900 US ad jobs were lost in the year to August (Emarketer). Separately, Digiday's closed-door townhalls report clients and senior staff passing on AI-generated briefs and slides with little review (one 18-page brief the client did not understand), longer briefs that are less clear, and "living briefs" as one fix.
- **Why us:** Hits the C-suite "embedding AI across our value chain" question from the inside. If AI writes the brief, the scarce skill is sharpening the question and reading culture first-hand, which is our cultural-insights capability. A fair position to take: AI behind the work, strategists owning the brief.

## Trends

**Creators are splitting revenue from reach: political creators earn on subscriptions while the big platforms give views but little money**  ·  `HIGH-IMPACT`
- **Source:** [Digiday — How political creators are juggling platforms to find the three Rs: reach, revenue and rules](https://digiday.com/media/how-political-creators-are-juggling-platforms-to-find-the-three-rs-reach-revenue-and-rules/), 3 Nov 2026
- **What:** Digiday spoke with six political creators and Syracuse's Center for the Creator Economy: no platform reliably gives reach, revenue and clear rules, so creators publish everywhere and monetise elsewhere. Aaron Parnas says about 97% of his revenue comes from Substack and about 1% from YouTube and Facebook, and 450M Instagram views in one month paid nothing. YouTube is the reliable earner (the article cites 55% ad-revenue share, unverified); Instagram gives reach but no Reels payouts; TikTok moderation of political content is erratic; Reddit is an emerging channel; Substack, beehiiv and Patreon carry the paid relationship. The sample is political news creators, so it may not generalise to lifestyle creators.
- **Why us:** Think Forward, as supporting evidence: creator value is migrating from platform views to owned, paid audience relationships, the same direction as the 11-02 Giggly Squad must-know. Brand implication: judge a creator partnership by the audience relationship they own across channels, not the reach of one post, and know which platform pays versus which one only reaches. A natural question for our creator-community work: what does a brand add when the creator's revenue already sits elsewhere?
