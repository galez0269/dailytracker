## Industry Buzz

**ChatGPT ads: fast growth, little visibility**  ·  `MUST-KNOW`
- **Source:** [Adweek — ChatGPT's Agent Writes Its Own Queries to Serve Ads. Advertisers Don't Get to See Them](https://www.adweek.com/media/chatgpts-agent-writes-its-own-queries-to-serve-ads-advertisers-dont-get-to-see-them/), c. 23 Nov 2026 (page undated; dated from Adweek's listing; partly paywalled); [Digiday — In Graphic Detail: How shoppers are adopting AI for product research ahead of Black Friday](https://digiday.com/marketing/in-graphic-detail-how-shoppers-are-adopting-ai-for-product-research-ahead-of-black-friday/), 25 Nov 2026; [Marketing-Interactive — Inside TAL's early push into ChatGPT advertising](https://www.marketing-interactive.com/you-do-need-an-internal-champion-inside-tal-early-push-into-chatgpt-advertising), 25 Nov 2026; [Digiday — Amazon is buying ChatGPT ads while blocking OpenAI crawlers](https://digiday.com/marketing/amazon-is-buying-chatgpt-ads-while-blocking-openai-crawlers/), 24 Nov 2026
- **What:** OpenAI's ad system uses an "orchestrator" agent that reads the chat (and past chats, if settings allow) and writes its own queries to match ads; advertisers see only aggregate reports, which one agency executive called very weak transparency. Brainlabs finds shoppers using AI for product research rose from 6% to 13% (2,800 US/UK shoppers); PMG found brand sites were only 24 of 203 links in AI answers recommending one client's products; AdClarity estimates ~$116.6M of ChatGPT ad spend in October. OpenAI says ads now run in 89 markets with 100,000+ advertisers; Australian insurer TAL reports cost per lead down about 90% (company figure). Amazon is buying ChatGPT ads for Black Friday.
- **Why us:** AEO is now partly paid, and the paid side is a black box. Our angle: the organic side still decides what AI says about a brand, and third-party sites, reviews and community conversation get cited far more than brand sites. That's social and creator work. Follows the 11-20 Business Hub item; a short "paid vs earned in ChatGPT" POV would land with clients reviewing Black Friday.

**No-AI clauses in creator deals; holiday ads go handmade**  ·  `MUST-KNOW`
- **Source:** [Digiday — Why brands are writing no-AI clauses into creator deals](https://digiday.com/future-of-tv/why-brands-are-writing-no-ai-clauses-into-creator-deals/), 24 Nov 2026; [Marketing Dive — Brands turn to handmade craft, handfuls of creators for holiday season](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/brands-turn-to-handmade-craft-handfuls-of-creators-for-holiday-season/875335/), 25 Nov 2026; [Marketing Dive — Chewy jumps on stop-motion animation trend](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/chewy-jumps-on-stop-motion-animation-trend-lighting-up-holiday-ads/875412/), 24 Nov 2026; [Marketing Dive — Geico unites brand icons in holiday ad mixing human craft, nostalgia](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/geico-unites-brand-icons-in-holiday-ad-mixing-human-craft-nostalgia/875728/), 25 Nov 2026
- **What:** Pearpop's VP of talent says more contracts now bar creators from using AI in campaign content, while still allowing AI for concepts, scripts and analysis; creators welcome it because AI content is crowding them out of feeds. He also advises capping paid usage at 3–6 months and never granting usage in perpetuity (one executive's view, from a Digiday event). On the creative side: Chewy's first stop-motion TV spot (no AI, handmade puppets, "Chewpanions" creators), Geico's stop-motion spot uniting the Gecko, Caveman and Maxwell (AI used only for pre-visualisation), plus Levi's claymation and Walmart's Muppets. Marketing Dive frames handmade craft as the answer to AI-ad fatigue, with creators at the centre of most holiday plans.
- **Why us:** The line from Coca-Cola (11-20) and Dior craftwashing (11-16) is now in contracts. Unfakeable capital in practice: people want to see human hands. Practical points for us: add a clear AI clause and usage terms to our creator contracts, and be ready to say where we use AI (making the work) and where we don't (the face of it).

**Creator agencies are the hottest acquisition target in advertising**  ·  `HIGH-IMPACT`
- **Source:** [Adweek — Want to Sell an Agency? Launch a Creator Marketing Firm](https://www.adweek.com/agencies/creator-influencer-agency-mergers-acquisitions-2026/), c. 25 Nov 2026 (page undated; dated from Adweek's listing)
- **What:** Quartermast counts 30 creator-agency acquisitions so far in 2026, against 20 in all of 2025 and the previous high of 28 in 2022, plus 124 creator-adjacent deals; examples include Samy buying Get Engaged and Billion Dollar Boy Group buying Creator Authority.
- **Why us:** Competitors are buying creator capability; we already have it built in. Use the number in pitches: clients should ask whether a creator team was built or bought last quarter. Agency-landscape thread.

**The clipping economy gets squeezed by platforms**  ·  `HIGH-IMPACT`
- **Source:** [Digiday — The clipping economy is changing as platforms crack down and creators seek control](https://digiday.com/media/the-clipping-economy-is-changing-as-platforms-crack-down-and-creators-seek-control/), 25 Nov 2026
- **What:** Paid clipping (fans or networks cutting creator content into short clips) costs about $1–4 per thousand views against $10–80+ for paid social (Clip Tech's co-founder). TikTok has tightened its originality rules, YouTube Shorts is cracking down on mass reposting and Instagram added a tool that finds copies of a creator's videos; Twitch is going the other way. Creators are bringing clipping in-house.
- **Why us:** Platform-feature fluency: cheap reach through reposts is closing. Brands that relied on clipping networks need original, native content and creator partnerships instead. Useful caution when clients ask about clipping as a cheap channel.

**Walmart lets Meta's AI agent shop its shelves, on Walmart's terms**  ·  `HIGH-IMPACT`
- **Source:** [Digiday — Walmart is letting Meta's Muse shop its shelves, but on its own terms](https://digiday.com/marketing/walmart-is-letting-metas-muse-shop-its-shelves-but-on-its-own-terms/), 24 Nov 2026
- **What:** A Walmart connector lets Meta's Muse agent search, build carts and check out with the shopper's permission, with optional spending caps of $50, $100 or $200; Amazon blocked Muse in September. One commerce agency head predicts brands will pay for "sponsored consideration" by agents.
- **Why us:** Shopping inside Meta's AI is the next step after social commerce. For FMCG clients, the question becomes how a product gets recommended by an agent, which is the AEO question again, inside Meta's platforms.

**Creator gifting is under ROI pressure**  ·  `SIGNAL`
- **Source:** [Glossy — As gifting to creators gains traction, brands worry about ROI and expectations](https://www.glossy.co/fashion/as-gifting-to-creators-gains-traction-brands-worry-about-roi-and-expectations/), 23 Nov 2026
- **What:** Some brands put up to 10% of marketing budgets into gifting (Modash survey), often with no post in return; Glossy's own research finds paid posts give more reach and gifted posts more engagement. Creators criticised Skims' $148 advent calendar, and one retailer is moving seeding money into in-store events.
- **Why us:** Gifting works when it's chosen and relationship-based, not mass mailing. We know creators; a short "seeding that earns posts" guide fits.

**Ring Concierge uses TikTok Live but sells only on its own site**  ·  `SIGNAL`

**Text posts make a comeback: Ocean Spray on Threads**  ·  `HIGH-IMPACT`
- **Source:** [Link in Bio — I'm throwing a premiere party for a deck](https://www.linkinbio.news/p/im-throwing-a-premiere-party-for), 24 Nov 2026 (paid; intro and one section read)
- **What:** Rachel Karten's deep dive asks whether text-based social is having a renaissance. **Gen Z performs a "work character"**  ·  `HIGH-IMPACT`
- **Source:** [idle gaze — 080: clocking in as a character](https://idlegaze.substack.com/p/idle-gaze-080-clocking-in-as-a-character), 25 Nov 2026
- **What:** Alexi Gunner argues Gen Z isn't overturning work culture but performing a tidier work persona. An Early Studies survey with idle gaze (392 open answers, US Gen Z, asked about their friends) finds 86% say friends become a different person at work, 30% hide their humour and 31% keep work off social media.
- **Why us:** Identity stacking in practice: people keep separate selves for separate places. For Think Forward, that means brands should speak to a context, not to "Gen Z" as one person. Small sample.

**Luxury turns founders into cartoon heroes, and fans fact-check them**  ·  `HIGH-IMPACT`

Glossy says the Dior post drew about 230,000 likes against a usual 20,000–30,000.
