## Industry Buzz

**ChatGPT ads: advertiser count up six-fold since June, but revenue per paid visit is $0.89 against Google's $2.45**  ·  `MUST-KNOW`
- **Source:** [Digiday — In graphic detail: Six thousand brands later, ChatGPT ads still have a conversion problem](https://digiday.com/marketing/in-graphic-detail-chatgpt-s-ad-growth-is-outrunning-the-proof-that-it-works/), 5 Nov 2026; companion: [Adweek — Brands Are Showing Up in ChatGPT. Proving It Pays Is Harder.](https://www.adweek.com/media/adweek-tech-advantage-brands-are-showing-up-in-chatgpt-proving-it-pays-is-harder/), 5 Nov 2026 (subscriber-only; standfirst and takeaways only)
- **What:** New since the 10-02 "budgets stuck at test level" item: third-party data now sizes the gap. Adthena tracked 1,082 brands advertising on ChatGPT in June, 6,333 in August and a projected ~6,830 in September. Shopping fell from 38% to 20% of US spend between April and September as financial services (2% to 13%), software and travel grew (Sensor Tower). Fewer than half of ad-eligible users see ads even in the UK (45.7%) and US (43.4%). A working paper under review (Kaiser and Schulze) finds paid ChatGPT visits generate $0.89 of revenue per session against $2.45 for paid Google Search, and an agency CEO says new measurement integrations still show attribution discrepancies. Adweek's companion piece says marketers have worked out how to get into AI answers but tying that to sales "is the hard part"; its 76% SMB figure is behind the paywall and not checked.
- **Why us:** The LLM-visibility conversation is moving from "how do we show up" to "what is it worth", and neither paid nor organic AI presence can yet show a clean return. Our angle: the inputs AI answers draw on (community proof, creator content, earned coverage) are worth building for brand reasons anyway, so treat AEO as a Proof investment, not a performance channel. Extends the 11-02 AI-shopping and 11-03 Reddit must-knows.

**AI across the value chain, both ends in one day: Fresh Step's AI-made ads at a tenth of the cost, and AI-written client briefs running to hundreds of pages**  ·  `MUST-KNOW`
- **Source:** [Marketing Dive — How Fresh Step used AI to go from brief to ads at one-tenth the cost](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/how-fresh-step-used-ai-to-go-from-brief-to-ads-at-one-tenth-the-cost/873688/), 5 Nov 2026; [Digiday — 'A little bit ridiculous': Agencies find AI overuse is leading to brief bloat](https://digiday.com/marketing/a-little-bit-ridiculous-agencies-find-ai-overuse-is-leading-to-brief-bloat/), 5 Nov 2026
- **What:** Clorox's cat-litter brand, losing relative perception on odour control, worked with AI-native agency RECE to take three 15-second spots (talking AI cats) from brief to finished creative in 30 days at one-tenth of traditional production cost; AI was used to prototype concepts mid-process so the client could "see and feel" them before committing. Ads run on linear TV with social cutdowns from 9 Nov; no results yet. On the same day, M&C Saatchi's North America CEO estimated that 85% of client briefs to her agency are AI-written, producing documents of up to hundreds of pages heavy on jargon and light on vision; a Basis survey (Jul-Aug) puts AI brief-writing at 56.4% of marketers and agencies. New since the 11-03 item (WARC strategists and the first townhall reports): a named figure on AI briefs and a costed production case.
- **Why us:** This is the answer to the C-suite "embedding AI" question in one pair: AI is excellent at compressing production and prototyping, and damaging when it replaces the thinking that defines the problem. A position we can own: AI behind the making, humans owning the brief and the cultural read. It also sets up the handmade wave in Trends as the other side of the same market.

**Serialised creator shows: Instagram's product VP calls a series "a goldmine" for the algorithm, and the economics are now public**  ·  `HIGH-IMPACT`
- **Source:** [Link in Bio — My call with Instagram's VP of Product](https://www.linkinbio.news/p/my-call-with-instagrams-vp-of-product), 3 Nov 2026 (paid; preview only) — Rachel Karten with Tessa Lyons; [Digiday — Future of TV Briefing: What it costs brands to fund long-form creator series](https://digiday.com/future-of-tv/future-of-tv-briefing-what-it-costs-brands-to-fund-long-form-creator-series/), 4 Nov 2026
- **What:** The visible part of Karten's recording (previewed in the 10-16 item) says Lyons described the algorithmic benefit of a series as "like a goldmine", and that brands should treat Instagram's longer video as "longer", not "long-form". Digiday adds the costs: a creator-led long-form series runs $150K to $5M; a package of 10 long episodes plus 30 shorts averages $300K-$500K, against around $5M for 10 episodes of unscripted TV. Talent typically holds the majority of the IP (60/40 or 70/30 with the studio), and brands usually do not own it but get specific assets and first refusal on later seasons. An LTK and Northwestern study says 57% of brands want creator campaigns to generate content.
- **Why us:** Platform-feature fluency with a price list: serial formats are what the platforms are now rewarding, and the brand's role is funder and collaborator, not owner. Compounding argument in practice (each episode builds on the last), and it follows the 10-16 branded-microdrama item. A useful client question: what do you get back if you do not own the IP?

## POVs

**Eugene Healey: distinctiveness, not differentiation; memory is "the only thing worth buying"**  ·  `HIGH-IMPACT`
- **Source:** [Considered Chaos — Post-Social Era :: Distinctiveness v. Differentiation Derangement](https://eugenehealey.substack.com/p/distinctiveness-v-differentiation-derangement), 4 Nov 2026 — Eugene Healey
- **What:** Differentiation is what a brand says it is; distinctiveness is what people remember, and in a feed of near-identical posts people remember the trend, not the brand (he cites System1 finding no correlation between engagement and brand memory). Distinctive assets go beyond the logo to a sound, a format or a real opinion; the job is to build attention the brand can pin to something only it owns, rather than rent it from the algorithm. Cases include KitKat's "Phone Break". His test: remove the logo from your last ten posts; would anyone know they were yours?
- **Why us:** Agree and extend: it is the creative half of platform-feature fluency. Features win the view, distinctive assets turn it into memory, and consistency over time is how both compound (Presence building into Power). The "logo off" test is a useful audit to run with clients. Continues his Post-Social Era series (10-28 entries).
